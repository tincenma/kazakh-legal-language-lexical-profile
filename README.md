# Lexical Profile of Kazakh Legal Language

Final project for **Programming for AI**, School of Artificial Intelligence and Data Science, Astana IT University
(educational program *Applied Artificial Intelligence*, group **AAI-2605M**). Cluster 3: Kazakh Legal Data Analytics, topic #17.

> **Goal:** study the vocabulary, n-grams and terminology of Kazakh legal texts and how they change across decades.
> **Focus:** Zipf's law, how stemming changes vocabulary size, and a decade-by-decade comparison.

## 1. Dataset

**KazakhLawCorpus-clean** (Hugging Face, CC BY 4.0):
<https://huggingface.co/datasets/Arailym-tleubayeva/KazakhLawCorpus-clean>

- One file, `data/kazakhlawcorpus_kk_only.csv`, about **337 MB**, with **215,889 acts** (one row per act) and 25 columns.
- Acts were adopted between 1947 and 2026.
- The corpus has **no full act text**. The text analyzed here is `title` + `description` (a short line such as "Order of the Minister of ... No. 417 of 15 July 2004").
- Main metadata columns: `law_id`, `date_adopted` (ISO `YYYY-MM-DD`), `is_repealed` (`t`/`f`), `act_form`, `legal_relation_area`, `legal_force`, `adopting_body`, `region` and `database_section`.
- The raw file is **not committed** because of its size. Menu item 1 downloads it automatically.

Data quality problems the audit handles:

| Problem | Example | How it is handled |
|---|---|---|
| Placeholders instead of missing values | `ЖОҚ`, `НЕТ`, `undefined`, `-` | converted to `NaN` and counted per column |
| Multi-valued fields | `legal_relation_area`, `act_form` joined with `", "` | split into tags; the many-to-many tables `act_areas` and `act_forms` |
| Commas *inside* a tag | `Нормативтік құқықтық актілер: дайындау, қабылдау, ...` | a piece starting in lower case is glued back to the previous tag |
| Repeated boilerplate titles | `... күшін жою туралы` ("on repealing ...") | removed from the test set if the same title is in train (no leakage) |
| `is_archive` / `is_repealed` stored as text | `"t"` / `"f"` | converted to the `boolean` dtype |

## 3. Pipeline

### 3.1 Loading large data (`data_loader.py`, requirement 11)
- Reads the CSV with `pd.read_csv(chunksize=50_000)` and cleans placeholders chunk by chunk.
- Cleans invalid values the audit found (`clean_values()`, logged in `cleaning_log.json`):
  - section names that are bare numeric codes (`3700`, `3800`, 19 acts) become missing;
  - repeated whitespace is collapsed in titles, descriptions and multi-valued fields.
- Optimizes dtypes: `datetime64` for dates, `boolean` for `t`/`f` flags, `category` for repeated metadata (body, section, form, region) and downcast integers.
- Measures memory **before and after** (`memory_usage(deep=True)`) and compares **CSV vs Parquet** on file size, read time and RAM.
- Results go to `reports/results/loading_benchmark.json` and `figures/01_loading_benchmark.png`.

### 3.2 Metadata audit (`audit.py`)
Reports missing values per field, raw date formats (ISO vs other), placeholder counts in the raw CSV, duplicate IDs and titles, acts per decade, and statistics for the multi-valued fields.
Results go to `audit.json`, `missing_values.csv` and `02_missing_values.png`.

### 3.3 Relational database (`database.py`, `sql/`, requirement 10)

```
bodies 1──N acts N──1 sections
              │
              ├──N act_areas N──1 areas     (many-to-many)
              └──N act_forms N──1 forms     (many-to-many)
```

The ER diagram is drawn to `docs/er_diagram.png`. `sql/queries.sql` holds **10 queries**, each loaded into pandas with `pd.read_sql_query` and saved to `reports/results/sql_*.csv`:

| Query | SQL features |
|---|---|
| `acts_per_decade` | GROUP BY, conditional aggregation |
| `top_bodies` | JOIN, GROUP BY / HAVING |
| `top_areas` | two JOINs over the many-to-many link |
| `multi_area_acts` | nested subquery in HAVING |
| `section_growth` | CTE + `LAG()` window |
| `body_rank_per_decade` | two CTEs + `RANK()` window |
| `cumulative_acts` | running `SUM() OVER` |
| `area_share_by_decade` | CTE + `IN` subquery + `SUM() OVER (PARTITION BY)` |
| `text_length_by_section` | JOIN + scalar subquery |
| `repealed_by_form` | JOIN + HAVING + `PERCENT_RANK()` |

The SQL results feed the analysis directly:
- five charts are drawn from the query DataFrames (`figures/15_sql_*` to `19_sql_*`);
- `summarize_findings()` turns them into the key facts in `sql_findings.json`, which are used in section 6.3.

### 3.4 Kazakh text preprocessing (`preprocessing.py`)
Kazakh is agglutinative: заң (law) → заңдар → заңдарының → заңдарынан. Three representations are compared:

1. **Word forms**: lower-cased Kazakh tokens after stop-word removal.
2. **Suffix stems**: a rule-based stripper that removes one suffix per group, from the outside in: case → possessive → plural → derivational. It also reverses consonant alternation (кітабы → кітап, министрлігі → министрлік) and keeps a short exception list (қазақстан).
3. **Character 3–5-grams**: sub-word units with word-boundary marks, which catch shared morphemes without any rules.

### 3.5 Lexical profile (`lexical.py`)
- **Vocabulary size** for each representation (types, tokens, type/token ratio, hapax share), showing how much stemming shrinks the vocabulary.
- **Zipf's law**: a rank–frequency log–log plot with a least-squares exponent *s* and R², for the whole corpus and for each decade.
- **Heaps' law**: vocabulary growth fitted as V = K·N^β, in chronological order.
- The most frequent words, **bigrams and trigrams** (NLTK), overall and per decade.
- **Decade comparison**: terms that rise, fall or first appear between consecutive decades (smoothed log ratio), word clouds per decade, and the history of any single term.

Decades with fewer than 300 acts are left out of the comparisons.

**Place-name filter.** Many acts are decisions of district councils, so village and district names would swamp the decade comparison.
- `extract_toponyms()` finds capitalized words placed right before an administrative unit word (*Шиелі ауданы*, *Достық ауылдық округі*).
- A candidate is kept only if the corpus almost never writes it in lower case. This keeps common words such as *еңбек* (labour) out of the list.
- The resulting 2,091 place-name stems (`toponyms.txt`) are removed from the decade shifts, per-decade top words, bigrams and word clouds. Vocabulary and Zipf statistics use all words.

### 3.6 Method comparison (`models.py`, requirement 9 and cluster tasks)
- **Task:** predict an act's `database_section` (its thematic section) from title + description.
- **Leakage-free split:** acts adopted **before 2018 go to train** and **2018 onwards go to test**. Test acts whose normalized title also occurs in train are **removed**. Sections with fewer than 200 training acts are dropped.
- **Models** (all except the majority baseline use the same `LinearSVC`, so differences come from the text representation):

| Model | Role |
|---|---|
| `baseline_majority` | always predicts the most common section |
| `baseline_wordform_tfidf` | TF-IDF on word forms, with no morphology handling |
| `stem_tfidf` | TF-IDF on suffix stems |
| `char_ngram_tfidf` | TF-IDF on character 3–5-grams |

- **Criteria:** **F1-macro** (main metric), F1-weighted, accuracy, fit and predict time, peak memory (`tracemalloc`) and number of features.
- The best model is saved to `data/processed/models/best_model.joblib`.

### 3.7 Error analysis (`error_analysis.py`)
Covers the confusion matrix, the most frequent *true → predicted* section pairs, error rate by text length and by year, sample errors (`error_examples.csv`), and the acts that only the word-form model or only the char n-gram model gets right.

### 3.8 Full-text case study (`fulltext.py`, menu item 10)
The corpus has no full act texts. adilet.zan.kz has no bulk download, and its robots.txt disallows the `/api/` that serves act texts, so the corpus is not crawled. Instead, the full texts of a small stratified sample are downloaded through the site's own interface:
- 96 acts by default, 3 per decade (1990s–2020s) in each of the 8 largest sections, listed in `data/fulltext/sample_to_collect.csv` with a collector assigned to each. Menu 10 → 1 changes the size; a larger list always contains the smaller one, so texts already collected stay valid.
- `fetch_pdfs.py` drives a real browser (Playwright), signed in with a team member's account. For each act on the list it opens the act's page, clicks **Жүктеу** → **PDF жүктеп алу**, and saves the PDF. Acts are fetched one at a time with a 4–8 s pause. The PDF text, with page-number footers removed, is saved as `data/fulltext/<law_id>.txt`. A text copied by hand into that file works the same way.
- For each act, the module compares the title + description with the full text: length, vocabulary, the effect of stemming, the share of title words that occur in the body, and the section the best model predicts from each.

The steps are described in `data/fulltext/README.md`.

## 6. Results

From `python main.py --run-all` on the full corpus (215,889 acts, Python 3.12, Windows 11).
All numbers come from `reports/results/` and all plots from `reports/figures/`.

### 6.1 Loading and storage

| Measure | CSV | Parquet |
|---|---|---|
| File size on disk | 321 MB | **63 MB** |
| Full read time | 4.2 s | **1.1 s** |
| Memory after reading | 606 MB | 512 MB* |

\*The Parquet file also stores the derived `text`, `year` and `decade` columns (28 columns instead of 25).

Optimizing the data types of the same 25 columns shrinks the in-memory frame from **617 MB** (every column as text) to **355 MB (−42.5%)**. Read times come from one run; two full runs differed by up to 20%.

### 6.2 Data audit
- `law_id` has no duplicates, but **54,389 titles are duplicates**, mostly boilerplate.
- **Only 53 acts were adopted before 1990.** Per decade: 1990s 9,455; 2000s 28,302; 2010s 91,706; 2020s 86,373. Decade comparisons therefore cover 1990–2020s.
- Placeholders were still present despite the dataset card: for example `regulatory_body` has 93,272 `ЖОҚ`, and `gov_registration_number` has 54,826 `НЕТ` and 23,948 `undefined`.
- `legal_relation_area` has 351 distinct tags. 152,674 acts have more than one (up to 12 per act).
- 19 acts had numeric codes (`3700`, `3800`) instead of a section name. They were set to missing.
- Repeated whitespace was fixed in 2,789 titles, 2,500 descriptions and about 1,000 values of each multi-valued field.

### 6.3 What the SQL queries show
Taken from `sql_findings.json` and the `sql_*` charts:
- **The corpus is recent.** 82.5% of all acts date from 2010 or later. The 2020s (2020–2026, still incomplete) already have almost as many acts as the whole 2010s.
- **Finance grows fastest.** The *Қаржы* (finance) section added 13,267 acts in the 2020s compared with the 2010s, even though the decade is not over. *Еңбек* (labour) has 4,461 fewer acts so far (`15_sql_section_growth.png`).
- **Regulation shifts toward budget and finance.** The general area "normative legal acts: drafting, adoption, amendment…" fell from 69–70% of area links in the 1990s–2000s to 44% in the 2010s–2020s, while state budget and finance areas grew (`16_sql_area_share.png`).
- **The Government is the largest single author,** with 37,807 acts (17.5%) (`18_sql_top_bodies.png`).
- **Technical documents are replaced most often.** *Регламент* (procedural regulation) has the highest repeal rate (91.9% of 4,161), followed by specifications, standards and instructions (`17_sql_repealed_by_form.png`).
- **Text length differs by section.** Social security, health and education acts have the longest texts (`19_sql_text_length_by_section.png`), which matters for the error analysis in section 6.7.

### 6.4 Vocabulary and the effect of stemming

| Representation | Types | Tokens | Hapax % | Types vs word forms |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| word forms (all) | 36,931 | 7.47 M | 34.4 | 100.3% |
| word forms (no stop words) | 36,829 | 5.86 M | 34.5 | 100% |
| **suffix stems** | **21,830** | 5.86 M | 35.6 | **59.3%** |
| char 3–5-grams | 109,725 | 126.7 M | 26.8 | 297.9% |

**Stemming reduces the vocabulary by 41%.** About 1.7 word forms map to each stem, which shows how much of Kazakh vocabulary growth comes from inflection.

### 6.5 Zipf's and Heaps' laws

| Corpus slice | Zipf *s* | R² |
|---|---|---|
| all word forms | 1.75 | 0.987 |
| all stems | 1.91 | 0.981 |
| 1990s | 1.27 | 0.992 |
| 2000s | 1.43 | 0.990 |
| 2010s | 1.67 | 0.987 |
| 2020s | 1.65 | 0.985 |

- **The fit is good but steep.** The distribution follows a power law closely (R² ≥ 0.98), but *s* is well above the classic 1.0. Titles and descriptions are short and formulaic, so a few legal terms take a very large share of tokens.
- **The exponent rises across decades.** Legal language became more standardized over time, as template acts by local councils grew in number.
- **Heaps' law:** V = 143·N^0.355. The vocabulary grows slowly, which is typical of a controlled register.
- **Top bigrams:** *қазақстан республикасы*, *аудандық мәслихатының*, *күші жойылды* ("repealed"), *мәслихатының шешімі* ("decision of the council"), *әділет департаментінде* ("at the justice department").
- **Place names grow sharply.** They make up 0.7% of content words in the 1990s, 3.7% in the 2000s, 9.1% in the 2010s and 10.5% in the 2020s. This confirms that district and village councils became a major source of legal acts, which also explains the rising Zipf exponent.

**Decade shifts after removing place names** (`reports/results/lexical_profile.json`):

| Period | Rising / new terms | Falling terms |
|---|---|---|
| 1990s → 2000s | *кесімдерді* (acts), *тізіліміне* (to the register), *заңнамалық* (legislative), *ратификациялау* (ratification), *паспорттарын*, *өңірлік* (regional) | Soviet-era abbreviations (*ҚРҮ*, *ҚРМК*), *кабинет* (Cabinet of Ministers), *контракт*, *борыштар* (debts) |
| 2000s → 2010s | *пробация* (probation), *одақтың* (Eurasian Union), *регламенттерін* (regulations), *жайылымдарды* (pastures), *қауымдық* (community) | *рыногын* (Russian-style "market"), *абоненттеріне*, *борышкерлермен* (debtors) |
| 2010s → 2020s | *қасбеттерін* (facades), *шатырларын* (roofs), *сәулеттік* (architectural), *келбет* (appearance), *пикеттеуді* (picketing), *сұрыптауға* (waste sorting), *жарна* (contribution) | *департаментінің*, *басқармасында*, *хаты* (letter), *интернаттық* (boarding school) |

The vocabulary moves from Soviet administrative terms toward registration and ratification (2000s), probation, Eurasian integration and procedural regulations (2010s), and urban-environment, assembly and social-contribution terms (2020s).

### 6.6 Method comparison
Split: train 107,441 acts (≤ 2017), test 90,597 acts (≥ 2018). 17,280 test acts were dropped because their title also appears in train. 33 sections.

| Model | F1-macro | Accuracy | Fit time | Predict time | Peak memory (fit) | Features |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| baseline_majority | 0.017 | 0.388 | 0.1 s | 0.0 s | 4 MB | – |
| baseline_wordform_tfidf | 0.573 | 0.765 | 63 s | 7 s | 59 MB | 18,812 |
| stem_tfidf | 0.572 | 0.762 | 31 s | 7 s | 51 MB | 10,916 |
| **char_ngram_tfidf** | **0.582** | **0.765** | 244 s | 177 s | 1,029 MB | 55,097 |

Times are wall-clock times from one run and vary between runs; memory and quality are stable.

- **Char n-grams win, narrowly.** They have the best F1-macro (+0.9 pt over word forms), but cost 4–8× the training time, about 25× the prediction time and 17× the memory.
- **Stemming matches word forms with fewer features.** It reaches the same quality with 42% fewer features and less memory, so it is the most efficient choice.
- **Recommendation:** use char n-grams when quality matters most, and stems when resources are limited.

### 6.7 Error analysis (char n-gram model)
- The error rate is **23.5%**. Most errors come from the broad section *Конституциялық құрылыс және мемлекеттік басқару* (constitutional order and public administration), which is confused with Finance, Labour and Security. Its acts cover every topic.
- Very short texts (6–10 tokens) fail **75%** of the time. Long texts (40+ tokens) also fail more often (30% vs 18–21%), because they mix several subjects.
- The word-form and char models disagree on only about 4% of test acts (1,756 vs 1,810 won by each), so the two representations make similar errors.

### 6.8 Limitations
- The corpus contains only titles and short descriptions, not full act texts.
- The place-name filter is heuristic. A village named after a common word that the corpus also writes in lower case (for example *Еңбек*) is kept as a normal word.
- Almost no data before 1990, so the "decades" are effectively 1990s–2020s.
- The stemmer is rule-based, so it sometimes over-stems (it merges *министрлік* and *министр*) and does not handle vowel harmony exceptions.
- The temporal split also shifts the class distribution between train and test, which lowers F1-macro for sections that are rare after 2018.
